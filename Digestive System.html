Introduction to the Digestive System



What is The Digestive System?

The digestive system is a group of organs that work together to digest and absorb nutrients from the food that you eat. It travels through the organs and is excreted as food waste. It breaks down and absorbs nutrients from the food and liquids you consume. This helps our body get all the nutrients it needs.





Types of Nutrients needed in Digestive System



Macronutrients

Macronutrients are the foundation of your body and are your main source of energy

Carbohydrates-the main source of energy that powers your body(bread,rice,pasta,grains,fruits,starchy vegetables,beans,milk,and yogurt)

Lipids-fats in your body that store energy, and digest fat(nuts,seeds,oil,butter,mayo,cream cheese)

Protein-helps build and repair muscles. Also helps regulate hormones(meat,fish,eggs,cheese,plain Greek yogurt, and tofu)

Micrts

Micronutrients are essential vitamins and minerals needed for the proper working of the body.

Micro-Nutrients include:

Vitamin A- support of immune system, metabolism and vision.(white rice, nutrient-dense breakfast cereal,black beans)

Vitamins B1-12- they help your body turn food into energy and support blood vessels, brain, and immune and nervous system.(milk,salmon,avocadoes,potatoes,eggs,white rice,beef)

Vitamin C- helps body grow tissue, heal wounds, and strengthen immune system(red peppers,oranges,grapefruits,and kiwis)


















How does the Digestive system work?


The Mouth


Digestion first starts in the mouth. This is called mechanical digestion. Mechanical digestion includes 3 main parts.


1.The teeth: Breaks down food into smaller pieces.

The saliva: Salivary glands make digestive juice called saliva that moistens the food to make it easier to swallow. They include an enzyme called amylase that helps break down starches and sugars.

The tongue:Mixes the saliva with the food.


The Esophagus


The Oesophagus: Food travels down the Oesophagus[also called the food pipe through a process called peristalsis]. It also passes through the Epiglottis(a small, leaf-shaped flap of elastic cartilage located in the throat behind the tongue and in front of the larynx (voice box) )



The Stomach


The stomach releases various acids and enzymes that help break down food further.Hydrochloric acid(HCL) helps kill bacteria and break down food. Pepsin is the acid that is in charge of digesting proteins. There's also a protective lining inside the stomach called Mucus. Mucus helps protect the stomach from the acid it releases.

The Small Intestine


The food travels from the stomach to the small intestine where digestive juices from the liver and pancreas called violin pancreatic juice complete the breakdown of proteins carbohydrates and fats. The juice from the liver called bile juice helps break down fats, juice from pancreas called pancreatic juice helps break down proteins. Bacteria in the small intestine make up some of the enzymes we need to digest carbohydrates. It moves water from your bloodstream into your gastrointestinal tract to help break down food.


The Large Intestine


Large intestine: More water moves from your GI tract into your bloodstream,and excess water gets absorbed. bacteria in the large intestine helps break down remaining nutrients and makes vitamin K.


The Excretory System

Any undigested food waste forms into stool and is stored in the rectum and is eventually released by anus. This process is called the excretory system.


The Gastrointestinal Tract


A muscular tube that runs from mouth to anus. The Upper GI tract consists of the mouth, esophagus, stomach, and the first part of the small intestine .The Lower GI tract consists of the small intestine, large intestine, rectum, and anus.
